sdlc-kit

A step-by-step way for AI to build software, with your approval at the checkpoints you choose.

AI tools that write software are fast, and they often skip steps. They guess what you meant and grade their own work. With sdlc-kit the AI works in six stages and writes down what it will do before it changes anything. At the checkpoints you keep, it waits for your approval.

Six stages, with checkpoints between them

At each stage the AI writes down what it found or will do, then stops at a checkpoint. By default a person approves three of them: the request summary, the change description, and the delivery.

  1. Understand request summary

    The AI looks around first and asks only what it cannot find out itself. It writes down what you want, marking what it checked and what it assumed.

  2. checkpoint · a person approves

  3. Describe now and after

    It describes how things work now and after the change, with a source for each fact. A second AI looks for gaps before you see it.

  4. checkpoint · a person approves

  5. Plan step-by-step plan

    It lists what will change, in what order, and how it will show that each part works. Nothing is changed yet.

  6. second AI reviews · risky work waits for you

  7. Build the changes

    It makes the changes in the planned order and checks each step as it goes.

  8. checks pass · goes on

  9. Deliver proof, delivery record, QA guide

    Separate AI reviewers test the result and collect proof. A person approves the delivery. The work counts as delivered only once delivery is confirmed. The last report gives you the account, the menu, and the steps to check it yourself.

  10. a problem appears later

  11. Follow up new request + lesson

    When something goes wrong after delivery, the AI finds the cause, writes the fix up as a new request, and records the lesson.

  12. starts over with a new request

A small, clear change takes a short route: understand, build, deliver. Everything else, and anything in doubt, goes through all six stages.

What you get

The AI follows written rules at every stage. Here is what they give you.

Approvals stay on record
Each approval is saved with what was approved, when, and how. If the approved text, or anything it was based on, changes later, the checkpoint closes until someone approves again. The approval for delivery covers the whole project as the reviewers saw it.
You choose where it waits
You can keep more checkpoints for a person, or hand all of them to the AI, which still reviews each one. The AI records your approval only after you say so plainly, and silence does not count. Risky work, such as deleting data, needs your OK first under every setting. What the AI reads along the way, such as a comment or a web page, counts as information. It takes instructions only from you.
A second AI checks the work
Reviews come from separate AI helpers that start fresh. They did not do the work and do not change it. They try the change as a user would, look for anything else it may have broken, and compare the result with your request. If no separate helper is available, that gap is written down. An optional mode adds a report with screenshots.
Proof behind every "done"
Every claim that something is done comes with real results from the project's own checks. A bug fix shows the problem happening, its cause, and the same steps working afterwards. If there is nowhere to try the change, the work is marked as not verified.
Every job ends with a clear result
Each job closes as delivered, dropped, stuck, or handed on to others. Delivered work needs proof of delivery. Dropped or stuck work must leave a written lesson for later jobs. On its own, the AI goes no further than handing its changes over for review, with your permission. Putting them into the live product is always a separate decision by a person.

What it cannot do

The kit notices when anything you approved is changed later, and closes the checkpoint again. It cannot prove who gave an approval, because anything that can edit the work can also edit the record beside it. The records stay on the computer where the work runs, so back them up. The kit does not replace your team's own testing or security review.

For the person who sets it up

This part uses commands. Three steps set it up in a project, and the README on GitHub has the details.

  1. git clone https://github.com/cskwork/sdlc-kit ~/sdlc-kit

    Once per computer. On Windows, run this and every later kit command in Git Bash or WSL.

  2. cd /path/to/project ~/sdlc-kit/init.sh

    Creates the record folder .sdlc/, kept out of git. Back it up, or keep it elsewhere with init.sh . --area <folder>. Your agent then fills in .sdlc/config.md and asks how many checkpoints stay human (lazymode).

  3. tell your agent: “read ~/sdlc-kit/AGENTS.md and follow it”

    Or add For SDLC work, read ~/sdlc-kit/AGENTS.md and follow it. to your tool's file below.
