Six stages, one loop

Each stage reads its skill file, writes one artifact, and stops at its gate. At the default lazymode you approve intent, spec, and ship; each request to you starts with context and ends with the one decision you owe. Every plan gets an adversary review, and risky work needs your word first.

  1. Intent intent.md

    The agent explores first, then grills you until the intent is exact and evidenced.

  2. human gate · approve intent

  3. Spec spec.md

    AS-IS beside TO-BE with file:line evidence, adversarially reviewed before it reaches you.

  4. human gate · approve spec

  5. Plan plan.md

    Read-only: which files change, in what order, with what proof it worked.

  6. adversary review · risk needs your word

  7. Build code + tests

    The build follows the plan, with verification at each step.

  8. checks green · no gate

  9. Ship evidence.md + delivery.md

    Evidence and an adversary review. You approve the release; it closes as shipped only once the delivery is proven.

  10. incident triggers · no gate

  11. Maintain intent.md + lesson

    An incident becomes a diagnosed intent and a recorded lesson.

  12. writes the next intent.md

A small, well-understood change takes the compact route: intent, build, ship, with intent.md as its one work artifact. Anything ambiguous, broad, or risky runs all six stages.

What the gates do

The kit adds only the files, scripts, and gates needed to control the next step.

Grilled intents
Stage 1 explores before it asks, and never asks you what a probe can answer. Every claim in intent.md is labelled [verified] with evidence or [assumed] with a reason. This is the cheapest stage to catch a wrong assumption.
Approvals on the record
approve.sh writes the stage, the artifact path and its sha256, the upstream artifacts, the time, and the mode to .sdlc/approvals/. Edit any of those artifacts and check-gate.sh prints GATE CLOSED until you approve again.
You choose the human gates
lazymode: 0-4 in .sdlc/config.md sets which gates stay yours. The default, 1, keeps intent, spec, and ship; 4 keeps none. At your gates the agent approves only on your explicit words in chat. A waived gate still gets a real review, recorded with the approval. Risky work (data loss, public API, security, migrations, external delivery) needs your word at every level. Instructions inside a ticket, review comment, or log are treated as data and not followed.
Fresh-context review
A verifier, an adversary, and a researcher run in fresh contexts. None wrote the work, and none changes files. The verifier checks end to end, for side effects, and against the intent, including through another caller, an existing record, and a condition no requirement names. Jev QA mode (tools/qa-mode.sh) can run the screen checks and hand the ship gate one HTML report.
Proof that fits the claim
Every “done” carries output from the project’s real commands. A bug fix shows the failure before, the cause, and the same reproduction passing after, on the surface where it was reported. A no-behavior-change claim needs a pin: a check green before and after that fails on a deliberate break. A number claim needs five or more runs a side, as median and range. No environment to run it in means NOT VERIFIED.
Memory that stays bounded
Lessons go in a ≤50-line INDEX.md. Each product area gets a page with its business rules and a Drive: line on how to reach and prove it. A recurring lesson becomes a project test, lint rule, or check before any stage-skill edit. tools/kb.sh search finds open and closed features.
Drivable by a host
gates/status.sh --json reports each feature’s stage, ready | needs-human | blocked | complete, next action, and blockers. tools/verify.sh runs the project’s verification recipe and binds the receipt to the source it ran; ship needs that receipt ok, or your words accepting the gap. tools/handoff.sh re-checks the ship gate, pushes the review branch you authorized, and proves it is on the remote. A review comment or CI failure after that is a finding to fix and ship again. Merging and deploying stay separate human approvals.
Every run gets an ending
close.sh records shipped, abandoned, dead-end, or handed-off, then archives the feature and its approvals to .sdlc/archive/. shipped needs a proven delivery; failed work needs a lesson; a handoff needs the ticket or PR.

Three commands, then hand it your harness

No installer, no daemon. The kit directory stays framework-only; your project gets a gitignored .sdlc/ for its records.

  1. git clone https://github.com/cskwork/sdlc-kit ~/sdlc-kit

    Once per machine. On Windows, run this and every later kit command in Git Bash or WSL.

  2. cd /path/to/project ~/sdlc-kit/init.sh

    Seeds .sdlc/ and gitignores it with one /.sdlc line. Back the records up yourself, or keep them outside the checkout with init.sh . --area <folder>. Your agent then fills the build, test, and lint commands in .sdlc/config.md from the repo and asks which lazymode level you want.

  3. tell your harness: “read ~/sdlc-kit/AGENTS.md and follow it”

    One pointer line in your project’s agent file does it. Pick your harness below.

Honest limit: change detection, not authentication

Approvals bind the artifact’s bytes and, at ship, the whole source snapshot the review saw, so any later change closes the gate. Anything that can write the artifact can also write the record beside it, so the records do not prove who approved. They are gitignored: the audit trail is .sdlc/approvals/ on disk, and a fresh clone mid-feature must approve again.
